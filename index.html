How it works

Three steps. No paper tokens, no change, no queue at the cash box.

  1. Top up once

    Guests scan the event poster and add money by UPI, or pay cash at the counter. Family members get their own sub-passes.

  2. Scan and pay

    At any stall, scan the stall’s QR or show yours. Tap an NFC band if you have one. The stall hears “₹80 received” in a second.

  3. Leftover goes back

    When the mela closes, unused balance is refunded to the UPI it came from — or donated, as the organizer announced up front.

FAQ

Questions organizers ask us

Is this allowed under RBI rules?

Tokenwala is built as a closed-loop token system. A pass works only at the stalls or counters of one event or outlet. It can’t be withdrawn as cash, sent to a bank, or spent anywhere else, and it earns no interest.

Guest top-ups are paid straight into the organizer’s own Razorpay or Cashfree account. Tokenwala is the software; it never holds, pools or settles guest money. RBI’s rules on prepaid payment instruments treat closed-system instruments very differently from open wallets.

We are having this structure reviewed by a fintech lawyer before live launch, and organizers remain responsible for their own GST, permissions and local rules. This answer is general information, not legal advice.

What happens to money left on a pass?

The organizer chooses a leftover policy before the event, and guests see it before they top up:

  • Refund — sent back automatically to the UPI or card it came from, newest top-ups first. Cash top-ups are refunded at the counter.
  • Donate — to a cause the organizer names.
  • Forfeit — only if clearly announced.
  • Keep until expiry — for restaurant and canteen passes, the balance stays usable until the pass expires.
What if the network drops at the ground?

Stalls keep taking payments. The stall phone works from a signed snapshot of passes with safe per-pass caps, and everything syncs as soon as signal returns. Offline payments are stored on the phone in a real database, so clearing the app can’t lose them.

If the guest’s phone is the one offline, they simply show their pass QR and the stall scans it.

Do guests need to install an app?

No. Scanning the event poster opens a web wallet that can top up, pay, show the balance and receive refunds. The Android and iPhone app adds Face ID and fingerprint approvals, Quick Pay and wallet passes.

What about children and guests without smartphones?

Parents can create family sub-passes with daily limits and blocked items. Children can pay with an NFC band. The cash counter can print QR cash cards, protected by a PIN, for anyone without a phone.

How do stalls get their money?

Stalls see every sale live. At close, Tokenwala prepares a settlement statement for each stall, with your commission, and you pay out from your own account. Mistakes can be undone within 15 minutes.

How safe is it?

Guest QR codes are signed, rotate every 30 seconds and work once. Bigger payments need Face ID, fingerprint or PIN. Quick Pay has caps and switches itself off on a new device or unusual activity. A lost phone can be frozen from the web. Every rupee moves through a double-entry ledger, reconciled nightly against the gateway and cash drawers.

Which languages does it speak?

The app runs in English, हिंदी and मराठी, and posters print in the languages you pick. This website is in English and हिंदी.
